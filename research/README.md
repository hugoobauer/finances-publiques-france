## Executive summary

1. **Purpose.** This folder is the evidence base for a French educational platform on public finances ahead of the 2027 presidential election. The platform's thesis is that many campaign proposals (the *taxe Zucman*, a return of the ISF, price freezes, retirement at 60) promise to fix public accounts or purchasing power without addressing the structure of public spending, and that the public misjudges orders of magnitude. The base is designed to be hard to discredit: every figure has a source, a unit, a year and a URL, and the strongest counter-arguments are set out in full.
2. **The user's three starting figures are all confirmed.**
   - Public expenditure 2025: **EUR 1,714.1 bn = 57.3% of GDP**.
   - Social protection benefits 2024: **31.9% of GDP** (Drees).
   - *Prélèvements obligatoires* (compulsory levies) 2025: **43.6% of GDP**.
   - Two nuances: (a) the 2024 levies ratio is **42.7%**, not 42.8%; (b) France is **no longer the EU's top spender**. Finland passed it in 2024 and 2025, and France is 2nd of 27.
3. **Size.** France's spending ratio is **7.6 points of GDP above the euro area (EA21) in 2025, about EUR 227 bn**. The gap peaked at 10.4 points in 2017.
   - About **two-thirds of the 2024 gap is social protection plus health** (46% and 19%). Old-age pensions alone account for about one-third. The claim that "pensions explain half the gap" is **not supported**; pensions plus health explain about half.
4. **The strongest caveat.** Measured **per inhabitant in purchasing power (PPS)**, France ranks only **8th of 27** (23,330 PPS in 2025). Its GDP per head is below the EU-27 average, which inflates every ratio to GDP.
   - Net of taxes and including private spending, France's social effort (32.0% of GDP, OECD 2021) is **below the United States' (33.2%)**. Against the European panel, though, France is still first.
   - On social protection benefits France is **2nd of 27 in 2024 and 3rd in 2025**, but the 2024 rank rests on a 0.05-point lead over Austria: say "among the top three".
5. **Deficit and debt.** The 2025 deficit was **EUR 151.6 bn = 5.1% of GDP**, so 8.8% of spending was borrowed. Debt was **EUR 3,460.1 bn = 115.7% of GDP**, 28.4 points above the euro area (it was 2 points *below* in 2005).
   - Interest rises from **EUR 65.7 bn (2025)** to **79.2 bn (2026f)** and about **91 bn (2027f)**.
   - Stabilizing the debt ratio requires about **3 points of GDP (EUR 90 bn)** of primary improvement.
6. **Proposals versus the hole to fill.**
   - *Taxe Zucman*: about **EUR 20 bn** according to its proponents, **EUR 2-6 bn** according to its critics.
   - A plausible post-behavior "tax the rich" package: **EUR 10-25 bn, i.e. 7-16% of the deficit**.
   - A general return to retirement at 60 costs **EUR 35-58 bn a year**.
   - None of the 2027 programs has been costed independently yet.
   - Popular "easy savings" targets are small. The net EU contribution (EUR 9.5 bn), the development-aid mission, the *Pouvoirs publics* mission, public broadcasting, all State payments to associations and the AME add up to about **EUR 29 bn gross, 19% of the deficit**, and most of it cannot go to zero (file 16).
   - Immigration: the OECD puts the net fiscal contribution of the foreign-born at **+1.0% to -0.85% of GDP a year**, depending on how public goods are allocated. The AME costs **EUR 1.34 bn** (2025). The RN's claimed 15.1 bn (2027) exceeds every official measure (file 15).
   - Macro feedback: official French institutions use a multiplier of about **0.6 in year 1** for a mixed package. A EUR 30 bn spending cut improves the deficit by only about EUR 16 bn in year 1 (file 17).
7. **The steelman is serious.**
   - Tax and contribution cuts decided in 2018-2023 cost **EUR 62 bn a year (2.2 points of GDP)**.
   - Over 2017-2024, revenue (ESA total revenue) fell by 3.1 points of GDP while spending fell by 0.7 points. Over 2019-2025, spending rose by 2.0 points.
   - Both readings are true. **The choice of base year decides which story you see.**
8. **Outcomes are mixed.**
   - Strong: treatable mortality (3rd lowest in the EU), low old-age poverty, low out-of-pocket health costs.
   - Average: overall poverty (16.3% = EU-27).
   - Weak: PISA 2025 mathematics (458, 29th of 38), employment rate (69.3%, 21st of 27), hours worked per inhabitant (last of 27).
9. **Perception.** No French poll since 2023 asks people to estimate a budget number, so the "misperception" premise rests on indirect evidence. For example, only 7% of people would cut pensions, which are 23.4% of spending.
   - Misperceptions also run the other way: fraud, immigration, *assistanat* (welfare dependency) and the State's "running costs" are overestimated too.
10. **Open work.**
    - All residual inconsistencies between files are fixed, including the four minor ones closed in the residual pass of 2026-10-25 (§8).
    - 58 groups of secondary items (about 74 items) are still unverified. None is a headline figure, and each has a recommendation (§7). Most should be dropped from the site or shown with a caveat.
    - Several releases due between December 2026 and June 2027 will replace headline figures (§9).

## 1. Purpose

- **What it is.** The reference base behind a public-finance education website for the 2027 French presidential campaign.
- **Thesis being tested.** France's fiscal problem lies mainly in the level and structure of public spending. Proposals that tax "the rich" or freeze prices cannot, on their own, restore public accounts. The public misperceives the orders of magnitude involved.
- **Standard.** Only official or recognized sources are used: Insee, Drees, DGFiP, DGAFP, Cour des comptes, HCFP, COR, CAE, HCSP, Banque de France, AFT, IPP, OFCE, Eurostat, OECD, IMF, European Commission. Think tanks are labeled with their orientation (Institut Montaigne, Fipeco, iFRAP, Rexecode, Institut de l'Entreprise, Terra Nova).
- **Rules applied in every file.**
  - Each figure states its value, unit, data year, publication date, institution and URL.
  - Every ratio is labeled **% of GDP** or **% of total public expenditure (TE)**.
  - Divergences between sources are explained.
  - The strongest counter-arguments (steelman) are given with their sources.
  - International comparisons cover EU-27, the euro area, the OECD and a panel (DE, IT, ES, NL, BE, SE, DK), with means, medians, ranks, 20-year gaps and outcome indicators.
- **Status date.** Everything is as of **2026-10-25**. The latest vintages used:
  - Eurostat October 2026 EDP notification (21 Oct 2026);
  - Insee 2025 accounts (May 2026, plus the August 2026 debt update);
  - PLF/PLFSS 2027 (1 Oct 2026) and HCFP opinion 2026-5 (25 Sep 2026);
  - COR June 2026;
  - Drees CPS December 2025 (the 2026 edition is not yet out);
  - PISA 2025 (8 Sep 2026);
  - OECD *Revenue Statistics 2025*.

## 6. Main points of disagreement among economists

| Debate | Strongest case against the thesis | Strongest case for the thesis | State of evidence | Where |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## 9. Known gaps and recommended next steps

### 9.1 Gaps in the research base

1. **No direct test of the misperception premise.** No French poll since 2023 asks people to estimate a budget number. The only numeric poll is Ipsos (January 2021: debt guessed at 76% of GDP). File 20 §6.4 recommends commissioning one.
2. **No independent costing of any 2027 program** (Montaigne, IPP, OFCE, Fipeco and the Cour had published none by 2026-10-25). The only external look is the Institut de l'Entreprise note on the RN (business-funded). The RN press kit itself is not online.
3. **Web-search budget ran out** during files 06, 08, 10, 11, 14 and 18. Some gaps may reflect search limits, not real absences.
4. **Blocked or browser-only sources:** OECD SDMX and publication pages, imf.org, Drees (firewall), budget.gouv.fr PDFs (browser session only; several *Jaunes* are transcribed, not saved), Légifrance, info.gouv.fr, rm.coe.int.
