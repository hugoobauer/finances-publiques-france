# Public Perception of Public-Finance Orders of Magnitude: What the Evidence Shows

*Research file 14. Prepared 2026-10-25. Reference base for a French educational platform on public finances ahead of the 2027 presidential election.*

> **Purpose.** The platform's core premise is that *the public misjudges orders of magnitude* in public finances, e.g. "taxing the rich would balance the budget". No earlier file sourced this premise. File 10 cited an "Ifop 86%" poll without verifying it and found no survey of economists. This file (1) collects representative French surveys from 2023-2026 and sets each result next to the true value from the research base, (2) reviews the academic literature on misperception and information effects on tax preferences, (3) checks whether any survey of French economists exists, and (4) builds the strongest counter-case: misperception also runs the other way.

> **Reading convention.** Every spending ratio says whether it is **% of GDP** or **% of total public expenditure (TE)**. Bracketed codes such as [S3] point to the numbered source list in Section 12. Survey results are percentages of respondents unless stated otherwise. "True value" refers to figures already verified in research files 01-10; the file is named in each case. Raw poll PDFs were saved under `research/data/raw/14_perception/`. The structured survey table is `research/data/14_perception_surveys.csv` (28 rows).

> **Search limit.** The session's web-search budget ran out partway through this task. Everything below was verified from primary documents fetched directly (poll PDFs, Drees, Insee, NBER, OECD, Cevipof). Items that could not be checked are listed explicitly in Section 10 and are never presented as facts.

## 0. Executive summary (for editors)

1. **The premise is supported, but the evidence takes a different form than the platform assumes.** **No representative French survey from 2023-2026 was found that asks people to *estimate a number*:** the share of pensions in spending, the size of the deficit, the debt in euros, or the yield of a wealth tax (Section 10). France has no French equivalent of the Ipsos "Perils of Perception" quizzes on the budget. The premise therefore rests on **indirect evidence**, which is strong but should be described as such.
2. **The strongest indirect evidence is a mismatch between where people want cuts and where the money is.** In September 2026 (Elabe for *Les Echos*, n = 1,005), the top three areas named for spending cuts were family allowances (34%), business aid (33%) and unemployment benefits (30%). Only **7% named pensions, 6% health and 5% education** [S5]. In 2024, family benefits were **4.0% of TE**, unemployment **2.9%**, old age **23.4%** and health **15.6%** (Eurostat COFOG, file 01). The two most-cited cut targets that have a COFOG line (family 4.0% + unemployment 2.9%) add up to about 7% of TE; business aid, the other top-three item, has no single COFOG line. The two items they protect most clearly (**pensions/old age + health**) make up **39% of TE**; adding education (8.9%) gives **about 48% of TE**, the figure used in table row 1 and §3.1(a) *(reconciled 2026-10-25: the summary's 39% and the table's 48% are both correct but used different lists; the items are now named in each place)*. Likewise, **67%** pick "cutting the State's *train de vie*" (running costs) as a top-3 lever to reduce debt, and in 2023 **72%** said savings should come from the State rather than from health and social spending (17%) [S5][S9]. Yet social protection plus health is **57.1% of TE** (file 01).
3. **A second mismatch: people want cuts in the abstract and reject them in practice.** In October 2025, **72%** said the 2026 budget effort should rely more on spending cuts, but only **29%** supported a pension freeze, and 21% supported doubling the medical deductible (*franchise médicale*) (Elabe, n = 1,001) [S3]. Odoxa for the free-market iFRAP found **82%** prefer spending cuts to tax rises [S2]. Ifop for the Socialist Party found **85%** want the budget effort to fall "more on the largest fortunes and companies" [S1]. **Both results are probably sincere. Question framing and sponsor drive the headline.**
4. **"Ifop 86%" is now verified.** Ifop for the *Parti socialiste*, fieldwork 10-11 September 2025, n = 1,000, online quotas. Wording: *"Créer une taxe de 2% sur les patrimoines supérieurs à 100 millions d'euros"* (create a 2% tax on wealth above EUR 100 million): **86% in favor** [S1]. The question gives no yield, no number of taxpayers and no mention of business assets. When Elabe described the tax as covering "1,800 households", including business assets, **59%** said MPs were wrong to reject it [S4]. That is still a majority, but 27 points lower (the two questions differ in format too).
5. **Perceived effectiveness, the closest proxy for "taxing the rich would balance the budget":** in October 2024, 79% judged higher taxes on very large, highly profitable companies "effective" for reducing debt, and 74% said the same of an exceptional contribution from the wealthiest (Elabe for *Les Echos*/Institut Montaigne) [S6]. Set against the true orders of magnitude, the Zucman tax raises EUR 2-25 bn depending on the method, or 0.1-0.8 point of GDP. The HCSP adjustment need is 4.4 points of GDP (files 07 and 10). People do not just *support* these taxes. They believe they work for the debt, which is the misperception the platform targets. **No survey asked respondents to estimate a yield.**
6. **Misperception also runs against the platform's own narrative (steelman, Section 7).** (a) **72%** think taxes have risen since 2017, yet the *prélèvements obligatoires* ratio fell from 45.3% to 43.6% of GDP (2017-2025). In nominal euros, however, levies rose about 26% (about 2.5% after inflation), so the perception is not simply wrong [S4]. (b) **59%** feel they "contribute more than they benefit". Insee finds **56%** of people are net beneficiaries of extended redistribution (2023 data) [S7][S10]. (c) **44%** think retirees are worse off than average. Retirees are at **100.2%** of the population average, or 106.5% with imputed rents (COR, file 03) [S11]. (d) French respondents put the immigrant share at **28.8%**, against an actual **12.2%** [S14]. (e) **82-84%** say the authorities do not fight fraud enough, while the realistic marginal yield of a new anti-fraud plan is EUR 1-3 bn a year (file 07) [S4]. **Misperceptions are not one-sided.** They feed "spending is the problem" narratives (fraud, immigration, *assistanat* (welfare dependency)) as much as "tax the rich" narratives.
7. **Information experiments show that facts move beliefs much more than policy views, with one big exception:** when people learn how few estates pay the US estate tax, support for that tax **more than doubles** (Kuziemko-Norton-Saez-Stantcheva 2015) [S15]. The French carbon-tax experiment (Douenne-Fabre 2022) found that correcting pessimistic beliefs raised support only modestly, because opponents discount favorable information [S18]. **For the platform this cuts both ways.** Correcting orders of magnitude is legitimate, but it should not be sold as something that will change votes.
8. **No representative survey of French economists on wealth taxes or fiscal consolidation exists**, as far as could be checked. The Clark Center European panel listing (checked 2026-10-25) shows no wealth-tax, consolidation or France question among recent polls [S20]. No AFSE survey and no *Le Monde* economists' poll was found (Section 6). Positions are known only from op-eds.
9. **Trust in fiscal information is very low**, which caps the effect of any fact-based platform: **87%** think public money is badly used and **87%** that the State is not transparent about it (Elabe, December 2025) [S7]. Only **22%** trust politics (Cevipof, January 2026) [S12]. The OECD finds France has the **lowest satisfaction** with social protection of France, Germany and the UK (28% vs 37-38%), despite having the most generous pension entitlements of the three [S19].

## 1. Scope, definitions and what counts as "misperception"

| Term | Meaning here | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Factual misperception** | A respondent's numerical estimate differs from the official value (e.g., perceived immigrant share 28.8% vs actual 12.2%). | This is the only direct test of the platform's premise. **No French 2023-2026 poll of this type was found for budget items.** |
| **Allocative mismatch** | People's stated priorities for cuts or for revenue fall on items that are small relative to the gap to close. | Indirect evidence. It can reflect values ("cut what I disapprove of") rather than ignorance of sizes. |
| **Perceived effectiveness** | Respondents rate a measure as "effective" for reducing debt. | The closest proxy for "taxing the rich would balance the budget". |
| **Framing effect** | The answer depends on the wording, the options offered or the sponsor. | Sponsored polls (PS, iFRAP, Montaigne) give opposite headlines. |
| *Prélèvements obligatoires* (PO) | Compulsory levies (taxes plus actual social contributions), Insee concept. | 43.6% of GDP in 2025 (file 05). |
| *Redistribution élargie* | Insee's "extended redistribution", which counts cash transfers **and** in-kind public services (health, education) as benefits received, and all taxes as contributions. | It is the official benchmark for the "net contributor" perception. |

**Representative-survey standard used.** These are commercial quota samples (Elabe, Ifop, Odoxa, OpinionWay), usually n ≈ 1,000 online, with a ±3-point margin of error at 50% (95% confidence). Drees uses n = 4,000 face to face. Academic panels use n ≈ 2,000-4,000 per country online. Quota online panels are standard in French polling, but they are not probability samples. Results among the least-connected groups are less reliable.

## 3. French surveys, 2023-2026: details

### 3.1 Perceived structure of spending: where people want cuts

**(a) Elabe for *Les Echos*, "Les Français et la dette publique", 3 September 2026** [S5]. Fieldwork 1-2 September 2026; n = 1,005 residents of metropolitan France aged 18+; online; quotas (sex, age, occupation, region, urban area). Raw: `data/raw/14_perception/elabe_dette_2026-09.pdf`.

- Q: *"Parmi les domaines suivants, selon vous, quels seraient les trois domaines sur lesquels il faudrait baisser la dépense publique ?"* (which **three** of these 16 areas should public spending be cut in? up to 3 answers) *[fact-check 2026-10-25: exact wording restored from the PDF]*. Results: family allowances (*allocations familiales*) 34%, business aid (*aides aux entreprises*) 33%, unemployment benefits 30%, culture 22%, environment 18%, defense 17%, transport 11%, counter-terrorism 10%, housing 8%, security 7%, **pensions 7%**, justice 7%, **health 6%**, long-term care 6%, **education 5%**, agriculture 4%; 15% "none".
- Q: preferred levers to reduce debt (3 of 10 items). Results: cut the State's running costs (*diminution du train de vie de l'État*) 67%; overall spending cuts 44%; higher taxes on the most profitable firms 37%; growth reforms 32%; cut social spending 32%; higher taxes on the wealthiest individuals 31%; more working time 13%; higher taxes on all firms 11%; less State funding for local authorities 10%; general tax rise on individuals 5%.
- **True values (file 01, Eurostat COFOG 2024, % of total public expenditure):** old age 23.4; health 15.6; education 8.9; family and children 4.0; unemployment 2.9; defense 3.2; culture/recreation 2.6; environment 1.8. Business aid has no single COFOG line. A narrow COFOG proxy (GF0401, general economic and labor affairs) is 2.7% of TE. Broad definitions (subsidies plus tax expenditures for firms) are much larger and disputed (file 08).
- **Reading.** The three most-cited cut areas (excluding business aid) total **about 7% of TE**. The three least-cited big items (pensions, health, education) total **about 48% of TE**. This could be a values judgment ("I want to protect pensions"), but it is consistent with the premise that people do not weigh items by size.

**(b) Elabe for BFMTV, "Les Français et l'utilisation de l'argent public", 3 December 2025** [S7]. Fieldwork 2-3 December 2025; n = 1,000; online; quotas.

- Q: should the level of spending be increased, kept or reduced? Increase: health 78%, security 66%, education 63%, justice 57%, counter-terrorism 56%, **pensions 53%**, defense 52%. Reduce: consultancies 77%, State running costs 75%, institutional communication 70%, agencies 52%, business aid 51%, civil-service headcount 50%.
- Compared with April 2019: defense +23 points, security +15, pensions -15 (towards "no change").
- **Reading.** A majority wants to *increase* spending on items that make up over half of TE, and to cut items that are, by any official measure, small (consultancies: the Senate inquiry committee reported that consulting spending by "the State in the broad sense" **exceeded EUR 1 bn in 2021**: EUR 893.9 m for ministries plus EUR 171.9 m for a sample of 44 State agencies (*opérateurs*), i.e. about EUR 1.07 bn, a minimum estimate since the sample covers only 10% of agencies; ministries' consulting spending more than doubled from 2018 to 2021 [S24]. *(verified 2026-10-25 against the Senate's own synthesis; was "about EUR 1 bn a year", which wrongly implied a recurring annual level: the figure is for **2021**.)* For scale: about 0.06% of 2025 TE (EUR 1,714.1 bn). The Cour des comptes later used a narrower scope and found lower amounts, so always state the scope.)

**(c) Ifop for *Acteurs publics* and EY (*Observatoire des politiques publiques*), July 2023** [S9]. Fieldwork 20-24 July 2023; n = 1,003; online self-administered; quotas. Reported results: 72% would look for savings in **State missions**, 17% in **health-social spending**, 11% in **local-authority missions**. Only **5%** proposed cutting pensions, and 5% public hospitals. 55% would cut RSA and integration spending, 41% family allowances. Exact question wording was not published in the article (⚠ re-checked 2026-10-25, still unavailable; recommendation: keep with caveat, report as "reported results" without quoting a wording).

- **True values:** social security funds 26.9% of GDP vs central government 22.8% (both unconsolidated, 2025, file 01). RSA plus *prime d'activité* sit in COFOG GF1007 "social exclusion n.e.c." at **2.2% of TE** (file 01).

**Reading.** People do not merely support wealth taxes on fairness grounds (a legitimate value judgment the platform should not dispute). They also believe such taxes are **effective against the debt**. That belief is where the orders-of-magnitude argument applies: even the author's static estimate (EUR 20 bn) would cover about 13% of the 2025 deficit (file 07). **Caveat:** "effective" in a poll is a loose word. Respondents may mean "it helps" rather than "it solves", and no poll asked "would it balance the budget?".

**Framing sensitivity of the Zucman question.** 86% (simple support, no detail, PS sponsor) vs 59% ("MPs wrong to reject", 1,800 households, business assets mentioned, media sponsor). The two numbers come from different question formats, so the 27-point difference cannot be attributed to information alone. It is nevertheless a sign that support falls when the base is described concretely.

**Reading for the platform.** French opinion is coherent on one point: **"someone else should pay"**. That means general tax rises (5%) and cuts to the big universal programs (pensions 7%, health 6%) are rejected, while taxes on the rich and cuts to "waste" are favored. That is exactly the configuration in which **orders of magnitude** matter: the measures with majority support are small relative to the gap, and the large levers lack majority support. This is the platform's premise stated in a defensible form.

### 3.5 Perceived tax burden and net contributions

- **Taxes since 2017** (Elabe/BFMTV, 4-5 November 2025, n = 1,000) [S4]. Q: *"Depuis la première élection d'Emmanuel Macron en 2017, avez-vous le sentiment que les impôts et taxes ont ... ?"* (since 2017, have taxes risen, stayed stable or fallen?). **72%** risen (44% a lot), up 14 points since October 2023; 18% stable; 10% fallen. True value: see Section 7.1.
- **Tax consent** (same poll): only **51%** agree that paying taxes is justified because it funds public services, down 10 points since March 2024 and back at the series low. **14%** agree that taxes are well used.
- **Net contribution** (Elabe/BFMTV, 2-3 December 2025, n = 1,000) [S7]. Q (paraphrased in the release): thinking about the taxes you pay and the services and benefits you can receive, do you contribute more than you benefit? **59%** contribute more, 23% as much, 7% benefit more. The share rises with age, from 37% (18-24) to 68% (50+). True value: Insee finds **56%** of people received more than they paid in 2023 (net transfer EUR 653 bn to net beneficiaries; share from 99% in the bottom decile to 18% in the top decile) [S10]. The figure was 57% in 2019 [S10b]. *[Fact-check addition 2026-10-25, from the IA 118 page:]* the net-beneficiary share is **96% among people in households whose adults average 65 or older**, against 43% for the rest of the population. Yet the feeling of being a net contributor *rises* with age (68% of those 50+). Insee also stresses that this redistribution "is not balanced": government levies less than it hands out, and public borrowing adds about EUR 1,400 per consumption unit (UC) on average. Part of the 56% therefore rests on debt-financed transfers. **Caveat:** Insee values health and education services at their production cost and counts pensions as transfers. Many respondents see pensions as deferred wages and may not count services as "benefits". The gap is partly conceptual, not purely factual.

**What the literature implies for the platform's premise.**
1. **Misperceptions of fiscal facts are common and large**: immigrant shares (×2.4 in France), estate-tax coverage (US), own rank, carbon-tax incidence (France). The premise that people misjudge orders of magnitude is **well supported in general**.
2. **Correcting them rarely changes policy preferences much**, except when the misperception concerns *who* bears a tax (the estate-tax case).
3. **Low trust in government is the main moderator**, and France ranks low on trust (Section 3.6). A platform that is seen as partisan will be discounted; one built on official sources has a better chance.

## 7. Steelman: misperception also runs the other way

The platform's thesis is that the public overestimates what "taxing the rich" can do. To be hard to discredit, it must acknowledge the misperceptions that point in the **opposite** direction, because they feed "spending is the problem" narratives that are just as weak on orders of magnitude.

### 7.1 "Taxes keep rising" (72%): wrong as a share of GDP, defensible in euros
- *Prélèvements obligatoires* fell from **45.3% of GDP (2017, series peak)** to **43.6% (2025)** (Insee, file 05). The OFCE attributes the 2017-2024 deficit increase, in accounting terms, to this fall (file 08). The Cour des comptes costs the cumulative tax cuts decided in 2018-2023 at **EUR 62 bn in 2023 (2.2 points of GDP)**.
- **But** in euros, levies rose from **EUR 1,038.1 bn** (2017) to **EUR 1,305.1 bn** (2025), i.e. +25.7% (Insee table 3.216, base 2020, 28 Aug 2026). *[Fact-check 2026-10-25: earlier text said the 2017 amount was "computed" as 45.3% × GDP. Insee publishes it directly, and the value is the same.]* Consumer prices (HICP) rose 22.6% over the same period, so **in real terms levies rose only about 2.5%**, while nominal GDP rose about 30%. (Data: `data/14_factcheck_PO_2017_2025_real.csv`.) Specific taxes did rise (energy excise, local property tax for owners). Individual experience can also differ from the aggregate (e.g. abolition of the *taxe d'habitation* (residence tax) on main homes benefited households, while the *taxe foncière* (property tax) rose).
- **Platform implication:** do not tell people "you are wrong, taxes fell". Say instead: "as a share of national income, compulsory levies are lower than in 2017. In euros they have grown, but more slowly than the economy and only slightly faster than prices."

### 7.2 "I pay more than I get" (59%) vs 56% net beneficiaries
This misperception supports demands for tax cuts and resentment of "*assistés*" (people on benefits). Most people underestimate the value of in-kind services: Insee counts health and education at cost [S10].

### 7.3 Fraud: overestimating the recoverable pot
- 84% / 82% say the fight against social / tax fraud is insufficient (Elabe, Nov 2025), and 92% backed the 2026 budget's anti-fraud measures (Elabe, Oct 2025) [S3][S4]. "Fighting fraud and waste" comes 3rd as a priority (41%, Elabe, Dec 2025) [S7].
- True values: estimated social fraud **about EUR 14 bn** "in the strict sense" (*fraudes évaluées au sens strict*), up from EUR 13 bn in the HCFiPS 2024 report, mostly because of base and scope effects; **about EUR 2 bn detected** and only **EUR 680 m recovered in 2024** [S25]. *(Verified 2026-10-25 against the primary HCFiPS annual note, adopted at its 4 Dec 2025 meeting and published 13 Jan 2026; was "EUR 13-14 bn a year (HCFiPS, via secondary press; primary not opened)".)* The HCFiPS itself writes that the topic is "*survalorisé au plan budgétaire*" (overweighted in budget terms): the amounts "are far from matching the needs for restoring the accounts", and "this point must be clearly stated and explained in the public debate". It also reports that about three-quarters of the French judge the fight against social fraud insufficient (71% Ifop for *Contribuables associés*, Jan 2025; 77% Elabe for BFMTV, Sep 2025). Other values (file 07): historical tax-fraud estimates range from EUR 17-26 bn (official, 1996/2007) to EUR 80-100 bn (a union estimate mixing fraud and avoidance). The **marginal** yield of a new anti-fraud plan is about **EUR 1-3 bn a year**. The HCFP called the government's EUR 1.5 bn figure "not credible", and it was dropped from the 2026 forecast.
- **Symmetry:** "fight fraud" plays the same role on the right as "tax the rich" on the left. It is a real issue with a static size that sounds large, but its realistic recoverable yield is small relative to the deficit.

### 7.4 *Assistanat*: overestimating minimum-income spending
- Respondents target family allowances (34%), unemployment (30%) and RSA (55% in 2023) for cuts. 77% back a cap on all social benefits (Odoxa/iFRAP), and 20% say the State does "too much for the most deprived" (Drees 2025, up from 9% in 2021) [S2][S11][S9].
- True values (% of TE, 2024, file 01): social exclusion n.e.c. (RSA, *prime d'activité*) **2.2%**, housing benefits **1.3%**, unemployment **2.9%**, family **4.0%**. All together, **10.4% of TE**, against **23.4%** for old age alone.

### 7.5 Immigration: overestimating numbers and transfers
- Perceived immigrant share **28.8%** vs **12.2%** (France, 2018 fieldwork). About 25% believe the average immigrant receives more than twice the transfers of a native. **Priming immigration reduces support for redistribution** [S14].
- 2026 politics: RN and LR programs claim immigration savings of EUR 8-15 bn a year, rising to EUR 29 bn by 2031 in the RN's case. Institut Montaigne and the Institut de l'Entreprise judge these legally fragile or undocumented (file 07). 68% support reducing non-contributory benefits for foreign residents (Elabe, Sep 2026) [S5].
- **The net fiscal contribution of immigrants** *(verified 2026-10-25; was "not verified in this session (⚠)")*. OECD, *International Migration Outlook 2021*, chapter 4, Table 4.1 [S26]: net fiscal contribution of the **foreign-born in France, % of GDP, 2006-18 average**: **+1.02%** counting only items attributable to individuals; **+0.25%** when congestible public goods are shared per capita; **-0.85%** when all pure public goods (including defense and debt interest) are also shared per capita. Intermediate variants in the chapter's annex: -0.30% (pure public goods except defense and debt charges) and -0.53% (except debt charges). For comparison, the **native-born** contribution on the last basis is **-3.25% of GDP**: under that convention both groups are net negative because general government ran a deficit. The OECD concludes that in France the net contributions of immigrants and natives are "more similar" than in most countries. A CEPII study cited by the OECD (Chojnicki, Ragot and Sokhna 2018) finds a net contribution to the primary budget between -0.5% and +0.5% of GDP over 1979-2011. **Platform rule:** always show the range (+1.0 to -0.9 point of GDP, depending on how public goods are allocated) and the native-born comparison, never a single "cost of immigration".

### 7.6 Pessimism about retirees and about society
44% think retirees are worse off than average; they are at parity (Drees 2025; COR). The French are too pessimistic about mobility. Only 17% expect an adequate pension, despite the most generous entitlements of the three OECD comparators [S11][S16][S19]. **This pessimism makes pension restraint harder to accept.** It works *against* the platform's spending-structure message, but it is also a misperception of orders of magnitude.

### 7.7 "Bankruptcy" alarm
60% think France is "close to bankruptcy" (Odoxa/iFRAP) [S2]. That overstates the immediacy of the risk relative to market indicators (file 06). A platform that warns about debt should avoid feeding a misperception in the opposite direction.

### 7.8 The strongest counter-arguments to the premise itself

1. **"It is values, not ignorance."** Supporting a wealth tax can be a judgment about fairness, regardless of its yield (Stantcheva 2021 [S17]). Calling it a "misperception" is then a category error. *Response:* the platform should separate fairness (legitimate disagreement) from sufficiency (a factual question: can it close the gap?).
2. **"The public is right about the direction."** 85% think debt reduction is urgent; 82% prefer spending cuts (Odoxa). On the big picture the public is not misinformed, only on the allocation of cuts.
3. **"Experts also misjudge."** Ex ante yields of the CDHR were overestimated by a factor of 3-4 by the government itself (file 07), and Zucman-tax estimates range from EUR 2 bn to EUR 25 bn. Uncertainty among experts is large. The platform should show ranges.
4. **"Correcting facts does not change minds."** The information-experiment literature (Section 4) predicts small effects on policy preferences, and none at all when trust is low.

## 9. How the platform should use this evidence

1. **State the premise precisely.** "Polls show that French voters favor measures (taxing the very rich, cutting State running costs, fighting fraud) whose realistic yield is small relative to the deficit, while they reject measures on the largest spending items. We found no French poll that measures numerical estimates directly." That is defensible. "The French think taxing the rich would balance the budget" is **not** directly sourced.
2. **Use the "where the money is" graphic.** Put the Elabe September 2026 cut list next to COFOG shares (table row 1). It is the single strongest piece of evidence.
3. **Apply the same treatment to every camp.** Wealth tax, fraud, immigration, "State running costs", and the EU contribution should all get the same orders-of-magnitude test (yields from file 07).
4. **Avoid sponsor-driven headlines.** Never quote a PS-, iFRAP- or Montaigne-commissioned poll without naming the sponsor and the wording.
5. **Consider commissioning a numeric-estimate survey** (n ≥ 2,000, quota or probability sample). Suggested items: share of pensions, health, education, defense, interest and foreign aid in TE; the deficit in EUR bn; the debt in EUR bn; the yield of a Zucman-type tax; the share of the deficit closed by it. Add a randomized information arm. This would fill the main gap identified here, and would be publishable.
6. **True values for the quiz** (from the research base): pensions/old age 23.4% of TE; health 15.6%; education 8.9%; defense 3.2%; interest 3.5% of TE in 2024 (COFOG GF0107) or 3.8% in 2025 (ESA D.41); **foreign economic aid (COFOG GF0102) EUR 4.9 bn = 0.3% of TE (2024)** (computed from `data/01_eurostat_gov_10a_exp_pctot.csv` and `..._mioeur.csv`). OECD-DAC official development assistance is a broader concept (it includes the share of the EU budget spent on aid, debt relief, in-donor refugee and student costs). *(Verified 2026-10-25; was "not verified".)* **France's ODA in 2025 (preliminary, grant-equivalent): USD 14.53 bn = 0.42% of GNI**, 5th-largest DAC donor by volume, down from 2024 [S27]. At the DAC's 2025 rate (0.8866 EUR per USD), that is about **EUR 12.9 bn, or about 0.75% of 2025 TE** (EUR 1,714.1 bn; own computation, mixing an OECD-DAC numerator with an ESA denominator, so indicative only). It includes USD 3.33 bn of multilateral aid through the EU and USD 1.13 bn of in-donor refugee costs.
