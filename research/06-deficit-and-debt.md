# 06 - Deficit, debt, interest burden, financing conditions and fiscal trajectory

*Research file for the French public-finance education platform. Status as of 2026-10-25. Every figure carries its source; all URLs accessed 2026-10-25 unless stated otherwise.*

---

## 0. TL;DR (for the platform editors)

- **2025:** France ran a Maastricht deficit of **5.1% of GDP (EUR 151.6 bn**, Eurostat October 2026 EDP notification; corrected 2026-10-25: was "about EUR 152 bn", the May 2026 vintage of 152.5 bn). Its debt reached **115.7% of GDP (about EUR 3,460 bn)**, about **EUR 50,000 per inhabitant**. Interest payments came to **EUR 65.7 bn (2.2% of GDP, about 3.8% of total public expenditure)**. The primary deficit, which excludes interest, was **2.9% of GDP**. To stabilize the debt ratio, a primary *surplus* of about 0.1% of GDP was needed (Cour des comptes). The gap was **about 3 points of GDP, or about EUR 90 bn per year**.
- **2026 (forecast):** The government's PLF 2027 puts the deficit at **5.4% of GDP**, up from a 5.0% target in the 2026 finance law, and debt at **119.3%**. General-government (APU) interest rises to **EUR 79.2 bn (2.6% of GDP)** in national accounts, on the government's PLF 2027 forecast (HCFP opinion 2026-5, §115). The Cour des comptes' June 2026 estimate on the same basis was EUR 77.4 bn (earlier vintage). The State's budget-accounting debt charge is a different, smaller concept (EUR 63.4 bn revised for 2026). At end Q2 2026, debt was already **EUR 3,595.5 bn = 119.0% of GDP** (Insee).
- **2027 (PLF, 1 Oct 2026):** The government targets a deficit of **5.0%**, debt of **121.7%** and interest of **EUR 91 bn (2.9% of GDP)**. The State's budget-accounting interest charge would be **EUR 72.9 bn**, above the **EUR 63 bn** defense military-programming envelope. The State plans to issue a record **EUR 340 bn** of medium- and long-term debt. The HCFP (fiscal council) calls the deficit ambition "a minimum" and the 2027 growth assumption "optimistic". It considers a return below 3% in 2029 "very unlikely".
- **Markets:** In September 2026 the monthly average 10-year OAT yield was **4.44%**. That was **97 bp above the Bund, 8 bp above Italy and 51 bp above Spain** (Eurostat). On 22 October 2026 it closed at **5.126%** (Banque de France TEC 10, end of day), above 5% for the first time since 2002 on Eurostat monthly data, with a spread of about **169 bp over the Bund** (TEC 10 minus Bundesbank 10-year yield; see section 3.4). Ratings stand at **A+ (Fitch, S&P, Scope)** and **Aa3 negative (Moody's**, affirmed 23 Oct 2026).
- **International:** France has the **3rd-highest debt ratio in the EU-27** (after Greece and Italy), the **4th-largest deficit in the EU-27** and the **2nd-largest in the euro area** in 2025. Its deficit has been **worse than the euro-area aggregate in every year from 2007 to 2025** (average gap -1.8 points of GDP against both the EA21 and the EA20 aggregate; re-computed 2026-10-25 from Eurostat `gov_10dd_edpt1`, 21 Oct 2026). The debt-ratio gap with the euro area went from **-2.0 points (2005) to +28.4 points (2025)** against the EA21 aggregate (canonical); against EA20, used in the tables below, -2.1 and +28.0.
- **For the platform's thesis:** The structural primary deficit is about 2-3 points of GDP, or EUR 60-90 bn a year. Interest is projected to add **EUR 10 bn per year until 2030** at unchanged policy (mission of economists, July 2026). These orders of magnitude are what any "taxing the rich" proposal has to be compared with. The yield of individual tax proposals belongs in file 03/taxation and is not computed here.

## 1. Scope and definitions

| Term | Definition used here | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| *Déficit public* / Maastricht deficit (net borrowing, ESA 2010 code **B.9**) | General government (*administrations publiques*, APU, sector S.13) net lending/net borrowing in national accounts (ESA 2010). It is notified to Eurostat under the excessive deficit procedure (EDP). | Covers the State, *ODAC* (central government agencies), *APUL* (local government) and *ASSO* (social security funds). It is **not** the State budget balance (*solde budgétaire*), which is cash-based and State-only. |
| *Solde budgétaire de l'État* (State budget balance) | Budget-accounting (*comptabilité budgétaire*) balance of the State's general budget, special accounts and annexed budgets. | Example: EUR -124.2 bn executed in 2025 vs. an APU national-accounts deficit of EUR -151.6 bn (corrected 2026-10-25: was -152.5 bn, May 2026 vintage). The scopes differ. |
| Primary balance | B.9 + interest payable (D.41). | Called *solde primaire* in French. |
| Structural balance | Balance corrected for the cycle and one-offs, in % of **potential** GDP. | Depends on the potential-growth estimate. The government, the Commission, the IMF and the HCFP all differ. |
| Maastricht debt (*dette au sens de Maastricht*) | Gross consolidated general government debt at nominal (face) value: currency and deposits, debt securities, loans (Reg. 479/2009, formerly 3605/93). | It is **gross**. Net debt (gross minus liquid financial assets) is about 7 points of GDP lower. |
| Interest burden (*charge de la dette*) | Two concepts. (a) **National accounts D.41 (interest payable)**, all APU, accrual basis, including the inflation-indexation provision on OATi/OAT€i. (b) **Budget accounting**, State only: *programme 117 "Charge de la dette et trésorerie de l'État"* (plus the SNCF Réseau debt taken over). | (a) is about EUR 10-15 bn higher than (b) because it covers all APU and records interest differently. |
| r - g | Average ("apparent") interest rate on debt minus nominal GDP growth. | If r > g, debt keeps rising unless the government runs a primary surplus (the "snowball effect"). |
| % of GDP vs. % of total public expenditure | Unless stated otherwise, every ratio is **% of GDP**. Where a figure is a share of total public expenditure, it is labeled "% of TPE". | |

Key observations:
- Between 2000 and 2025 France met the 3% threshold in only **6 of 26 years**: 2000, 2001, 2006, 2007 (exactly 3.0), 2018 and 2019. Eurostat data.
- The **primary balance has been in deficit every year since 2002**. Interest fell from 3.0% of GDP in 2000 to 1.3% in 2020 even though debt doubled, because rates were falling. That "interest dividend" (about 1.7 points of GDP) has now reversed: 2.2% in 2025, 2.9% forecast for 2027.
- **Verified 2026-10-25 (Insee primary series):** the last year in which French general government (APU, Maastricht/ESA 2010 basis) ran a **surplus was 1974**: +EUR 0.199 bn = **+0.1% of GDP**. APU has been in deficit **every year since 1975 (51 consecutive years through 2025)**. Earlier surplus years were 1958-1966 and 1972 (1970 was -EUR 0.04 bn, 0.0% of GDP). Wording for the site: "France's public accounts have not been in surplus since 1974", not "the State has not voted a balanced budget since 1974": on the same national-accounts basis the State subsector alone was already in deficit in 1974 (-1.1% of GDP), and the 1974 APU surplus came from social security (+0.6%) and central-government bodies. **PLF 2027 content (1 Oct 2026)**
- The plan contains EUR 43 bn of new consolidation measures (EUR 25 bn spending, 18 bn revenue). Including measures already decided, the total is EUR 54 bn.
- Measures include: State ministry budgets frozen in nominal terms except defense; ONDAM capped at +2.0%; no indexation in 2027 of certain pensions and of some social benefits (housing aid *APL*, *prime d'activité*), with the most modest retirees, disability and minimum-income benefits still indexed to inflation (corrected after verification: was "basic pensions frozen except the lowest"; the PLF text says "non revalorisation des prestations sociales (APL et prime d'activité) et de certaines pensions"); civil-service index point frozen; and limits on local-government operating spending.
- At unchanged policy, the government says the deficit would have widened by about 1 point of GDP in 2027.
- The structural adjustment is 0.5 points of potential GDP; the primary structural adjustment is 0.8; the primary structural effort is 1.2.
- **HCFP measure:** structural effort of 0.9 points of GDP (about EUR 28 bn), of which EUR 17 bn on revenue and EUR 11 bn on spending. The primary spending effort is EUR 23 bn, of which EUR 12 bn is absorbed by the rise in interest.

**EU framework**
- **Excessive deficit procedure (EDP):**
  - 19 Jun 2024: Commission report under Art. 126(3).
  - 26 Jul 2024: Council decision on the existence of an excessive deficit.
  - 21 Jan 2025: Council recommendation under Art. 126(7). It sets **2029** as the deadline and a corrective **net expenditure path**: maximum nominal growth of 0.8% (2025), 1.2% (2026), 1.2% (2027), 1.2% (2028) and 1.1% (2029), i.e. +9.5% cumulative since 2023.
  - The same path is in the national medium-term fiscal-structural plan (*PSMT*) endorsed by the Council. It runs over 7 years (2025-2031), with the deficit expected at 2.4% in 2031 under Commission assumptions. The Council projected debt at 122.6% of GDP at end 2029 on this path.
  - Sources: Council recommendation ST-5033-2025-REV-1 (https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-5033-2025-REV-1/en/pdf); Commission EDP page for France (https://economy-finance.ec.europa.eu/economic-governance-framework/stability-and-growth-pact/corrective-arm-excessive-deficit-procedure/excessive-deficit-procedures-overview/france_en).
- **Compliance:**
  - The HCFP warns that 2026 net expenditure "could exceed the ceiling".
  - For 2027 the government projects net expenditure growth of +0.7%, below the +1.2% ceiling.
  - The HCFP judges that respecting the path in 2028-29 **would not suffice** to get below 3% in 2029.
  - **Verified (2026-10-25):** the Council note ST 11122/26 (3 Jul 2026) states that "based on the Commission's assessment on effective action of 3 June 2026, the excessive deficit procedure for France is held in abeyance". The Commission document is cited there as COM(2026) 200 final (corrected after verification: was cited as COM(2026) 302 and left unverified). ## 7. Economists' disagreements and the strongest counter-arguments (steelman)

The platform's thesis is that debt and deficits are a structural problem that tax-the-rich or price-freeze proposals do not address. Below are the strongest objections, with sources, and how official institutions answer them.

1. **"r < g: debt can be rolled over at no fiscal cost"**
   - *Argument:* Blanchard, "Public Debt and Low Interest Rates", AEA presidential address, *American Economic Review* 109(4), 2019. When the safe rate stays below nominal growth, debt ratios fall without primary surpluses. France enjoyed r < g for most of 2015-2024.
   - *Status 2026:* the Cour des comptes and the HCFP both state that **r now exceeds g**: 2025 r = 2.0% vs g = 1.9%, and new issuance at 3.35-4.4% vs projected nominal growth of 2.2-2.7%. Blanchard himself stressed that the argument applies to *low and stable* r. The steelman is therefore weaker now than in 2019. It still has force if the yield shock of 2026 proves temporary.
2. **"Austerity is self-defeating: multipliers are large"**
   - *Argument:* Blanchard & Leigh, "Growth Forecast Errors and Fiscal Multipliers", IMF WP/13/1 (2013), found multipliers well above 1 in 2010-11. OFCE (Policy Brief 154, Feb 2026, https://ofce.github.io/budget2026/) estimates multipliers of 0.7-0.8 on structural spending cuts and about 1 on defense spending and the *prime d'activité* (in-work benefit). In its estimate, the 2026 budget cuts growth by 0.5 pt.
   - The HCFP itself judges the 2027 growth assumption optimistic partly because of fiscal drag. The IGF mission concedes that getting below 3% by 2029-31 requires the "very favorable" assumption of a **zero multiplier**.
   - *Answer by official bodies:* the Cour des comptes argues a "chosen" consolidation is less costly than one "imposed by market pressure", citing Portugal and Italy. The HCFP (2026-5, table 18) lists past consolidations of 1.1-2.7 pts of GDP per year in Spain, the Netherlands, Portugal, the UK and Sweden, and of 0.7-0.8 pt per year in France itself (1994-98, 2011-15) (corrected after verification: was "about 1 pt of GDP per year in Spain, the Netherlands, Sweden and France itself").
3. **"Good debt finances investment"**
   - *Argument:* French public investment is high at 4.4% of GDP vs 3.7% in the EA20. The 2025 primary deficit (2.9) is *smaller* than gross public investment (4.4). Climate and defense needs justify borrowing. This is the golden-rule debate: Blanchard, Leandro & Zettelmeyer (2021); the 2024 EU reform allows longer adjustment periods conditional on investment and reforms.
   - *Counterpoint:* the 2025 overall deficit (5.1) exceeds gross investment, and the gap is larger net of depreciation. The PLF's own "main investment expenditures" are only EUR 26-38 bn a year.
4. **"Debt is not a crisis: the market is deep and maturity is long"**
   - *Arguments:*
     - Average maturity of 8.5 years (Cour des comptes), so rate shocks pass through slowly: +100 bp adds only EUR 3.4 bn in year 1.
     - The OAT market is among the deepest and most liquid in the euro area.
     - Net debt is about 7 pts below gross.
     - Rating agencies still put France in the A+/AA range.
     - The ECB's Transmission Protection Instrument (TPI) exists as a backstop. But its first eligibility criterion is "compliance with the EU fiscal framework: not being subject to an excessive deficit procedure (EDP), or not being assessed as having failed to take effective action in response to an EU Council recommendation under Article 126(7)" TFEU (ECB press release "The Transmission Protection Instrument", 21 Jul 2022, https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2022/html/ecb.pr220721~973e6e7273.en.html; verified 2026-10-25, was "from memory"). France is under an EDP, held in abeyance since June 2026 after a positive effective-action assessment (section 3.1), so it currently meets the second branch; a finding of non-effective action would remove it.
     - Japan (about 197% gross liabilities, OECD EO 119) and Italy (about 137% Maastricht) have carried higher debt for decades without default.
     - Moody's affirmed Aa3 (negative outlook) on 23 Oct 2026, its scheduled review date (AFT ratings page). (Corrected 2026-10-25: the earlier claim that the affirmation was "against market expectations" is **dropped**; CNBC only says investors "were anticipating" the decision.)
   - *Counterpoints:*
     - France's gross financing need is >20% of GDP (EC DSM) and EUR 340 bn of issuance is planned for 2027.
     - Non-residents hold **59.7% of negotiable State debt** (Q2 2026, market value; AFT Monthly Bulletin no. 437 / Banque de France). In Japan, overseas investors held **13.1%** of government bonds and treasury bills at end June 2026 (JPY 150 tn of 1,142 tn, market value), or 7.9% of bonds excluding bills (Bank of Japan, *Flow of Funds, 2nd quarter 2026, preliminary*, Chart 6-2, Sep 2026, https://www.boj.or.jp/en/statistics/sj/sjexp.pdf). (Corrected 2026-10-25: was "58% of the debt ... against about 10-15% in Japan (not verified here)".)
     - France does not issue in its own central bank's currency in the way Japan does.
     - Italy illustrates the "low-growth trap" of a debt stabilized at about 140% with heavy primary surpluses (Cour des comptes).
5. **"Bond-market stress is a political, not a fiscal, phenomenon"**
   - *Argument:* the 2024-2026 spread widening coincided with the dissolution (June 2024), the fall of governments and budget gridlock. Moody's own rationale cites "fragmentation of France's political landscape". On this reading, stability rather than austerity would lower spreads.
   - *Counterpoint:* the UK 2022 "mini-budget" episode, when gilt yields jumped and the Bank of England had to intervene in late September 2022, shows how quickly a credibility shock can become a financial-stability event. Verified 2026-10-25: on **28 Sep 2022** the Bank of England announced temporary purchases of long-dated gilts "on whatever scale is necessary" to "restore orderly market conditions", run from 28 Sep to 14 Oct 2022 and indemnified by HM Treasury, because disorder in the gilt market threatened UK financial stability (Bank of England, "Bank of England announces gilt market operation", 28 Sep 2022, https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/news/2022/september/bank-of-england-announces-gilt-market-operation). (Was "from memory".)
6. **"Spending is not the only lever; revenue matters"**
   - *Argument:* the Cour des comptes notes that the 2025 deficit reduction came **exclusively from EUR 23 bn of tax increases** (corrected after verification: was "mainly"; Cour RSPFP June 2026: "Ce sont exclusivement les hausses d'impôts de 23 Md€ ... qui expliquent la nette réduction du déficit public en 2025"), while spending grew slightly faster than GDP. Compulsory levies are forecast to rise from 43.6% of GDP (2025) to 44.2% (2027, PLF). Proponents of wealth taxes argue revenue measures with low multipliers, such as taxes on high wealth, are the least growth-damaging way to consolidate.
   - *Counterpoint:* the IGF mission states that margins on revenue are "reduced" given levels "already very high" and tax competition. It also says it would be "illusory" to concentrate the effort on "certain categories only of the population". Spending, chiefly pensions and health, must be "questioned". The size of the gap (EUR 90-125 bn) is the key comparison for the platform. The yields of specific taxes are covered in the taxation file.
