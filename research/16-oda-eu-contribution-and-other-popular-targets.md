# 16 - Popular "Easy Savings" Targets: Development Aid, the EU Contribution, Agencies, Consultants, Politicians, Public Broadcasting, Associations, AME

*Research date: 2026-10-25. Canonical denominators come from [00-reference-figures.md](00-reference-figures.md): total public expenditure (TE) 2025 = **EUR 1,714.1 bn** (row 1); Maastricht deficit 2025 = **EUR 151.6 bn** (row 31); GDP 2025 = EUR 2,991.1 bn (Eurostat `nama_10_gdp`, provisional). Where an amount is a 2026 or 2027 budget figure, its "% of TE" and "% of deficit" are taken against the 2025 outturn denominators, so they are **orders of magnitude, not exact ratios**. Against the 2027 deficit forecast (EUR 158.1 bn, row 34) every "% of deficit" below would be about 4% smaller.*

## 0. Key takeaways

1. **None of these targets, even abolished outright, closes the deficit.** Taken at their full gross value, the EU net contribution (9.5), the development-aid mission (3.2), the whole "*Pouvoirs publics*" mission (*Présidence*, Parliament, Constitutional Council: 1.2), public broadcasting (3.8), all State payments to associations (10.5) and AME (1.2, programme 183 credits) add up to about **EUR 29 bn, i.e. about 19% of the 2025 deficit and 1.7% of TE**. Most of that cannot legally or practically go to zero.
2. **Realistic yields are much smaller:** the Senate puts recurrent savings from reorganizing State agencies at about **EUR 0.54 bn** (vs. 2-3 bn claimed by the government in 2025); strategy consulting spending is already down to **EUR 60 m** (2025); the net EU contribution is set by a unanimous treaty-level decision, so its unilateral yield is about **zero**.
3. **The one large line is "operators"** (EUR 72.6 bn of public funding in 2027), but 39% of their operating subsidies go to universities, and most of the rest funds research, employment, training and housing policies, not "agency overhead".
4. **France is not an outlier on these items** except public broadcasting and publishing (COFOG 08.3: 0.26% of GDP, 5th of 27, vs. EU-27 0.20%). On development aid (0.42% of GNI) it sits in the middle of the DAC and below its northern peers; on the EU budget it is the 2nd-largest net contributor in euros but 4th as a share of GNI.

### 3.3 Why the net balance understates and overstates

The Budget Directorate itself warns that the net balance "*ne saurait à elle seule retracer la totalité des coûts et bénéfices de l'appartenance à l'Union*" (cannot by itself capture all the costs and benefits of membership): some EU spending (external action, pre-accession) cannot be allocated to countries, and single-market gains are not counted [S3, p. 156]. A proposal to "cap the net contribution at EUR 5 bn" would, even if legal, save at most 1.4-4.5 bn on 2025 figures; legally the contribution follows from the Own Resources Decision adopted unanimously and ratified by all member states (file 11, §4).

## 11. Where economists and institutions disagree

1. **"Small items matter symbolically."** Proponents (e.g. iFRAP; parties of the right and RN) argue that cutting visible running costs is a precondition for public consent to larger reforms, and that small lines add up. **Counter:** the Senate rapporteur (C. Lavarde, LR) said that "*tous ceux qui annoncent des milliards d'euros d'économies en parlant des agences mentent un peu aux Français*" (Public Sénat), and the adjustment needed is about 4.4 points of GDP (files 07 and 10), or about EUR 125 bn by 2032 according to the Cour des comptes and the IGF (file 11).
2. **Net EU contribution.** Sovereigntist parties treat the net balance as a cost; the Budget Directorate and most economists treat it as an incomplete indicator that ignores single-market gains and non-allocated spending [S3].
3. **ODA.** NGOs (Coordination SUD) argue the 2027 cut (-43% in commitments) is the largest ever and the sixth consecutive reduction [S15]; budget hawks note that ODA in France already includes loans and in-donor costs, so budget cuts overstate the fall in actual aid. The OECD's 2025 data show a broad fall in DAC aid [S1].
4. **Agencies.** The government (2025) and the Senate disagree by a factor of 4-6 on achievable savings; iFRAP argued the Senate report "can go further" (title of its July 2025 note, not opened ⚠).
5. **Public broadcasting.** Supporters of privatization cite the 0.26%-of-GDP spending level (above EU average); opponents cite pluralism and note that most of the saving would be one-off sale proceeds.
